# Surface playbooks

Per-surface detail behind the register table in SKILL.md section 3. Read the row
you need. The three bans and the invariants apply on all of them.

## LinkedIn post

80 to 200 words. One idea. One number if a real one exists.

Open on the concrete thing rather than on a claim about the industry. Nobody
needs the state of coaching software explained to them in line one.

Structure that works for him: the observation, the number, what he is unsure
about or still looking for, then the invitation.

Ban list on top of the standard three: no hook-line-then-blank-line-then-payoff
formatting, no numbered listicles, no "Here's what I learned," no "Unpopular
opinion," no single-sentence paragraphs stacked into a wall for rhythm. That
stacking is parataxis wearing a layout.

He can say he is a student and a founder in the same breath without hedging
either. He should never write a post that could sit on a hundred other feeds.

Close by asking for something real, since he is genuinely still interviewing
coaches.

## LinkedIn DM and cold outreach

Under 90 words. The ask lives on its own line.

Say who he is in one clause, why he is writing to this specific person in one
sentence, and what he wants. A DM that opens with flattery reads as a template.

Never claim to have read something he has not read. When he has read it, name
the specific part.

No "I'd love to pick your brain." No "quick question" when the question is not
quick.

## Email: coaches, users, customers

Short. Second person. He is talking to somebody whose time is billed by the
hour.

Lead with the answer or the change. Support it underneath. If something broke,
say what broke, what he did, and what they should expect, in that order.

Never apologize twice. One specific apology beats three general ones.

## Email: professors, advisors, investors, PPG

Assume the recipient holds all the context already, which is his most emphatic
standing instruction. Do not restate the study, the meeting, the thesis or the
problem back to the person who owns it.

Structure: what changed or what he found, the number, the one thing he is unsure
about, the ask. Sign off with his name alone.

Formal register still means direct. "I could be wrong on the windowing" is more
credible than a paragraph of hedging, and it is what he actually writes.

Attachments and results get named in the body, never left to the reader to
discover.

For research and clinical recipients, every claim carries its source or gets
cut, and no number appears that is not in the data.

## Product and website copy

**Read the product's own `BRAND.md` and `DESIGN.md` first.** They are the
authority and this file is not. They carry the positioning line, the names that
have been retired and must not come back, the ICP with its exact qualifiers, the
recurring phrases to reuse rather than reinvent, and the measured sentence
shapes.

What holds whatever those files say:

- headings of three to six words, verb-first where possible, second person,
  present tense, capability stated flat with no intensifier
- the honesty rule, since a product in pilots has no metric, logo or outcome to
  invent
- the strongest true claim the company has is the one that leads, stated the way
  it was learned rather than as a benchmark that was run

If a sentence could sit on a hundred other product sites, it does not belong.

## Research and grant writing

Length follows the content, and the person is first person plural.

Every claim carries a source or a number. Vague attribution of the "prior work
suggests" kind gets replaced by the actual citation or deleted.

Method, then result, and then a limitation stated in one sentence that does not
apologize for itself.

The three bans still apply. Academic prose is where negative parallelism hides
most comfortably.

## Docs and repo files

Imperative. State the rule, then the reason it exists.

His own BRAND.md and DESIGN.md are the model: numbered hard rules at the top,
the reasoning underneath, and a note when a previous version was wrong and got
rewritten. That note is worth keeping, because it stops the old version coming
back.

Mild profanity is acceptable in an internal doc and pointless in a public one.

## Slack, iMessage, PR bodies

Slack and iMessage follow his private register with his co-founder, and stay
professional
with anybody else.

PR bodies say what changed and why, in that order, at whatever length the diff
earns. Commit messages get one line unless the change genuinely needs more.
